2. Power levels: single-phase vs three-phase

Residential Mode 3 chargers in NZ are commonly supplied at 230 V single-phase or 400 V three-phase (line-to-line). The on-board charger limits what the vehicle accepts.

SupplyTypical installer settingApprox. AC powerNotes
1-phase, 16 A16 A circuit (Mode 2 path 7.9.3.2 uses 20 A min.)~3.7 kWLower demand; not full 7.9.3.3 single-phase 32 A path
1-phase, 32 A32 A dedicated subcircuit~7.4 kW7.9.3.3(a) minimum capacity for single-phase Mode 3/4
3-phase, 16 A / phaseSubcircuit sized per load (e.g. 20 A or 32 A per phase per design)~11 kWCommon “fast AC” residential cap; verify EVSE SDoC
3-phase, 32 A / phaseHeavy demand; submain and mains upgrade often required~22 kWMaximum typical residential AC; full max-demand study essential

Important distinction: Clause 7.9.3.3(a) explicitly requires a minimum 32 A current-carrying capacity for single-phase final subcircuits. It does not state the same numeric minimum for three-phase. Three-phase installations must still comply with 7.9.3.3(b)–(f), general cable sizing (Section 3 / AS/NZS 3008), maximum demand (2.2.2), and WorkSafe principles (equipment rated 230 V or 400 V as applicable).

WorkSafe’s Jan 2026 addendum notes that with load management, a 32 A (7.5 kW) charger can often satisfy daily driving without exceeding typical domestic peak demand — but this is not permission to skip design.

3. Mandatory NZ rules (Clause 7.9)

3.1 Clause 7.9.3.3 — Domestic Mode 3 and Mode 4 (verified text)

RefRequirement
(a)Single-phase final subcircuits: minimum 32 A current-carrying capacity
(b)Final subcircuit shall supply no other point in the wiring
(c)Separate Type B RCD 30 mA, all live conductors (active and neutral); RCBO allowed
(c) exc.Type A 30 mA + RDC-DD per IEC 62955 (≥ 6 mA DC detected)
(d)Direct connection (fixed wiring) — not a general-purpose socket circuit
(e)Isolator ≥ 32 A, adjacent to charging facility (Clause 2.3.2.2.1)
(f)Socket-outlet or EVSE cable ≥ 800 mm above floor/ground
NOTEIf equipment > 20 A single-phase: consider mains upgrade and/or load management

Source: AS/NZS 3000:2018 Amendment 1, Clause 7.9 (NZ only) — electrotraining / Standards extract.

3.2 Other Clause 7.9 items

  • 7.9.2.2: Socket-outlets shall not be installed for Mode 1 charging.
  • 7.9.3.1: New domestic installations with defined integral garage shall provide a Mode 2 or Mode 3/4 charging facility.
  • 7.9.3.2: Mode 2 — 20 A minimum dedicated subcircuit; approved socket types; 800 mm height.
  • 7.9.4: Non-residential — Type B (or Type A + RDC-DD for Mode 3) on final subcircuit.

4. Protection: Type B vs Type A + RDC-DD

BoardEVSECompliance path
2-pole Type B RCBO/RCD 30 mAAny compliant Mode 37.9.3.3(c) — simplest
2-pole Type A RCBO/RCD 30 mABuilt-in IEC 62955 RDC-DD (verify SDoC)7.9.3.3 exception
EVSE with integrated Type BPer manufacturerWorkSafe addendum: no duplicate Type B required at board, but subcircuit still needs RCD coverage per 3000 note

Protection must cover the entire final subcircuit from origin to EVSE (cable between board and charger included). Single-pole devices are not acceptable where all poles must be protected.

RCD function testing per modified 8.3.10 must include DC leakage checks where applicable (2025 ESR). This verifies installation — not factory certification of the RDC-DD.

5.1 Mode 2 vs Mode 3 location note

Mode 2 (IC-CPD plugged into socket) is discouraged by WorkSafe for employer vehicles and high sustained current. Mode 3 fixed EVSE is the preferred residential solution for 32 A equivalent charging.

7. Recommended locations and examples

7.1 Suitable location checklist

  • Within dedicated garage or carport bay — wall facing vehicle charge port
  • Exterior wall near parking with weather-rated EVSE (IP55+ if exposed)
  • Close to MEN main switchboard when practical (short subcircuit, clear max-demand path)
  • Protected from vehicle contact (bollard, wheel stop, offset from door travel)
  • Ventilation and manufacturer temperature range met
  • Distributor notification completed if required

7.2 Worked location examples

Example A — Integral garage (new build): Mode 3 wallbox on rear wall of garage, 1 000 mm AFFL, 400 mm from internal corner, IP44 minimum (dust/moisture). Dedicated 32 A subcircuit from garage MSB (MEN). Type A RCBO + EVSE RDC-DD. Vehicle parks nose-in; Type 2 tethered cable reaches port without crossing pedestrian door path. Complies: 7.9.3.3, 7.9.3.1 facility provided.
Example B — Carport (no full enclosure): IP55 wallbox on house wall facing carport slab, 900 mm AFFL, conduit in wall from MSB 6 m away. Concrete-mounted bollard in front of charger. Load management set to 16 A evening peak. Complies if IP, height, protection, and max demand documented.
Example C — Driveway beside house: Three-phase 11 kW EVSE near meter/MSB; 3-pole Type B RCBO; subcircuit sized 20 A/phase (or per EVSE). Height 850 mm; cable route in UV-stable conduit above ground level. 7.9.3.3(b)–(f) apply; 32 A single-phase rule (a) N/A to 3-phase.
Example D — What to refuse: Customer requests charger inside ensuite bathroom Zone 2, 600 mm from basin. Decline — wet zone prohibition; propose external garage or exterior IP-rated location outside classified zones.
Example E — Detached garage: 25 m submain; EVSE on front wall. Confirm MEN/outbuilding rules before choosing garage DB as subcircuit origin. RCBO at origin of final subcircuit; 32 A isolator at EVSE; submain sized for 32 A + other garage loads. See Section 10 Example 5.

8. Cable, submain, and capacity

  • Size cable per AS/NZS 3008.1.1: installation method, grouping, ambient, length.
  • Voltage drop: typically ≤ 5% from origin to load (AS/NZS 3000 / 3008).
  • Single-phase 32 A: many installs use 6 mm² Cu for short runs — always calculate; do not copy blindly.
  • Three-phase: size each phase conductor for design current; neutral and earthing per 3000.
  • Maximum demand (2.2.2): EV often at full rated current unless proven assessment, limitation, or LMS.
  • Notify electricity distributor when required by network rules.

9. Testing, CoC, and records

  1. Visual: location, height ≥ 800 mm, IP rating, labelling, dedicated circuit, isolator position.
  2. Section 8: continuity, insulation, polarity, earth fault-loop impedance where required.
  3. RCD operation including DC leakage per ESR-modified 8.3.10.
  4. EVSE operational checks per manufacturer (earth monitoring, etc.).
  5. Record energization date at switchboard (2018 edition).
  6. Issue Certificate of Compliance; attach EVSE SDoC to CoC (EWRB good practice).

10. Worked installation examples

1. Single-phase 7.4 kW (32 A) — MSB in garage
63 A supply; 8 m run; Type 2 wallbox with RDC-DD; Type A 2-pole RCBO 32 A; 6 mm² V75; LMS throttles to 20 A when oven on. CoC references 7.9.3.3 and LMS settings.
2. Single-phase — old 50 A board, no spare capacity
Install 3.7 kW (16 A) on dedicated 20 A Mode 2 facility OR Mode 3 with LMS cap 12 A overnight OR supply upgrade. Do not fit 32 A hardware without assessment.
3. Three-phase 11 kW (16 A/phase)
400 V supply; 3-pole Type B 30 mA; cable 4 mm² per phase (verify); EVSE SDoC 400 V 50 Hz; max demand includes other 3-phase loads. 7.9.3.3(a) not applicable; (b)–(f) are.
4. Three-phase 22 kW (32 A/phase) — high-end home
Distributor upgrade likely; 10 mm² or larger per phase per 3008; main switch and submain upgraded; fixed load management mandatory; CoC documents full calculation.
5. Detached garage subboard
Verify MEN switchboard origin per WorkSafe; size 25 m submain; protection at final subcircuit origin; local isolator at EVSE; 8.3.9 outbuilding tests.

11. Master installer checklist

  • Design to AS/NZS 3000:2018 + Amendments 1, 2, 3
  • Site survey: restricted zones (Section 6 wet areas, impact, hazardous)
  • Height ≥ 800 mm; IP/IK suitable for environment
  • Dedicated final subcircuit; direct connection Mode 3
  • Type B 2-pole 30 mA OR Type A + IEC 62955 RDC-DD (SDoC on file)
  • Subcircuit from MEN switchboard (confirm detached garage case)
  • Single-phase: 32 A min. subcircuit capacity; three-phase: sized per load + 7.9.3.3(b)–(f)
  • 32 A isolator adjacent to EVSE
  • Max demand / volt drop / cable size calculated (3008)
  • Load management or upgrade if sustained 32 A exceeds capacity
  • SDoC, IEC 61851, 230/400 V 50 Hz confirmed
  • Section 8 tests + DC leakage; CoC issued
  • Distributor notified if required
